# When Trend Tree first had evidence

**Five of 2,513 accepted Trend Hunter articles matched an original publisher URL in the Trend Tree comparison.** Trend Hunter picked up all five earlier, by a median of **41.98 hours** (range 31.36–52.36). By November 20, 2026 at 00:00 UTC, one had supported a candidate that merged into an existing trend. None supported the original candidate for a newly promoted trend. Three had been attributed to four active trends. The five establish an article-level timing difference. Independent verification of new facts and a discovery advantage require separate evidence. [Provider cohort and collection method](crma-1912-trend-hunter-article-profile.md) · [Research ticket](https://mcclatchy.atlassian.net/browse/CRMA-1913)

## What each clock establishes

| Event | Clock and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Publisher publication | TIE `published_at` describes the publisher's article. Trend Tree `SIGNAL_TIMESTAMP` has source-specific meanings. Discovery uses an extracted publication date or the run clock, Google Trends RSS uses the trending query's pubDate, and GDELT uses its own seendate. [TIE field definitions](https://nxt.trendhunter.com/api/integrations/tie-signals/v1/docs) · [Discovery writer][discovery] · [RSS parser][rss-clock] · [GDELT writer][gdelt] |
| Trend Hunter observation | TIE `first_seen` records pickup. The API serves the current article verdict without a selection timestamp. In a prospective pilot, record when the poll first receives the eligible article. Historical pickup estimates the earliest possible availability through an accepted-only feed. [Provider timing check](crma-1912-trend-hunter-article-profile.md#timing-and-verdicts) |
| Trend Tree observation | Earliest retained `STG_EXTERNAL_SIGNALS.INGESTED_AT` for the same article is the first documented staging receipt. `FCT_SIGNALS.EMBEDDED_AT` records when its embedded fact became available. The five-minute task skips null titles, excluded sources and invalid embedding inputs. A delayed or absent fact can coexist with an earlier staging receipt. [Staging DDL][stg] · [Embedding writer][embed] |

Flatten `SUPPORTING_SIGNAL_IDS` and join its values to `FCT_SIGNALS.SIGNAL_ID` to identify which evidence underwrote the candidate. The current writer builds both the array and source counts from resolvable facts. The winning candidate can have earlier, separately recorded candidates about the same movement. | New evidence for an existing trend | Earliest `FCT_TREND_SIGNALS.LINKED_AT` across kinds for the article–trend pair records the first persisted association. `supporting` is materialized from promoted or deduplicated candidate arrays. `attributed` records the sweep's accepted association. Count each pair once, preserve link kind, and inspect the earlier evidence for a substantively new fact. Enrichment citations have their own `PAYLOAD:evidence` and ledger `WRITTEN_AT`. ## Measured through the UTC cutoff

The accepted cohort contains every article first seen from November 13 at 00:00 UTC through November 20 at 00:00 UTC, end exclusive. The baseline used staging receipts from October 1 at 00:00 UTC to the same cutoff. It yielded **43,723 distinct literal original URLs**, 27,073 first observed in October and 16,650 in November within that lookback. We compared SHA-256 of the exact UTF-8 URL strings locally. Three discovery sources retain `METADATA:original_url`. Google Trends RSS and GDELT retain `METADATA:url`. The other **2,508** accepted articles had no literal match under these conditions. Earlier receipts, other sources, URL changes, syndicated copies and different reporting on the same movement remain possible. [Discovery fields][discovery] · [RSS fields][rss-url] · [GDELT fields][gdelt] · [Provider cohort](crma-1912-trend-hunter-article-profile.md)

For the five matches we read **each table as of the cutoff** with `AT (TIMESTAMP => '2026-11-20 00:00:00 +00:00'::TIMESTAMP_TZ)`. All five were embedded. Four entered through ChatGPT discovery and one through Google Trends RSS. They came from four publisher hosts, including two PR Newswire URLs. Three articles accounted for four `attributed` pairs on four already-promoted trends. All four trends were active at Trend Hunter pickup, native intake and link time. One of those articles also supported a merge onto a fifth, dormant trend. Two matched articles had no recorded candidate or trend link. An attribution establishes the production job's judgment of relevance. Whether the article added independently verified, previously unknown facts remains open. Snowflake query IDs `01c88a59-071b-235c-001b-328754a41976` (cutoff links), `01c88a5a-071b-26f1-001b-328754a40496` (arrival and link states) and `01c88a5b-071b-2825-001b-328754a3f7fe` (pickup states). [Attribution selection][attr-select] · [Snowflake Time Travel](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-time-travel)

**Worked example.** Trend Hunter picked up the matched Beverage Industry article on November 16 at 05:45:27 UTC. Trend Tree received it on November 18 at 10:07:08 UTC and embedded it at 10:07:16. Candidate `cand-ofzvmcetmwm4foro` recorded it at 12:52:40. The ledger recorded `MERGE_INTO_EXISTING` at 14:15:24. The target had already been promoted on October 7 and was dormant. Its supporting link landed at 14:18:53. A separate, active trend received an attributed link on November 19 at 05:30:16 UTC. The 52.36-hour pickup lead belongs to the article. The known decision was a merge, and the active-trend link is eligible for independent evidence review. [Provider collection method](crma-1912-trend-hunter-article-profile.md#repeatable-request-recipe) · [Merge writer][merge] · [Supporting writer][support]

## Reproducing an as-of comparison

1. **Fix the cutoff and time zones.** Parse provider timestamps with their explicit offset. `SHOW PARAMETERS LIKE 'TIMEZONE' IN SESSION` returned `America/New_York` at account level. For warehouse-generated NTZ clocks above, use `CONVERT_TIMEZONE('America/New_York','UTC', clock)` and a half-open UTC interval. Source-supplied publication or signal timestamps follow their writers' conventions. Surviving rows with event times inside the measured week included 6,938 staged rows, 84 candidate inserts, 18 minted identities, 80 supporting-link rows and 1,298 attributed-link rows across Trend Tree. Those are pipeline volumes. Snowflake query ID `01c88a58-071b-2825-001b-328754a3f55a`. [Snowflake timestamp types](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/sql-reference/data-types-datetime#timestamp-ltz-timestamp-ntz-timestamp-tz) · [Session-clock evidence][timezone]
2. **Establish the identity.** Match TIE's original `url` to the two raw URL fields used by the five sources above. Retain the resulting native `SIGNAL_ID` for all downstream joins. A second pass can use redirects and the writer's actual canonicalization, with a separate match label. RSS normalizes its feed URL. Discovery follows redirects and normalizes the final URL. GDELT's search writer can key a result by a hash. Require provenance for syndication or common ownership before treating copies as independent. The staging merge is first-writer-wins on the signal key, with ties inside one batch resolved by signal timestamp and array index. Later arrivals can fill an empty claim field. Their source, metadata and receipt time disappear as separate observations. [URL rules][canon] · [Discovery redirects][redirect] · [RSS writer][rss-url] · [GDELT writer][gdelt] · [Staging merge][first-writer]
3. **Reconstruct eligibility at the time.** Main distillation considers unclaimed discovery signals ingested in the preceding seven days. Its caps are 320 per source, 1,600 total, with a 150-row floor. Revisit has a separate 48-hour pool of previously claimed signals. The cluster agent requires at least two distinct in-pool signal IDs. Families derive from `SOURCE_BREAKDOWN`. Amazon and Google Trends variants collapse into their respective families. The three discovery agents retain distinct families. An unrecognized source name defaults to its own family. Two families route normally. A single family scoring at least 0.5 on confidence and specificity can take the Exploding Topics corroboration path. Preserve one article across intake channels and one family per platform. Provider acceptance, a second feed label and a wire reprint add no independent confirmation on their own. [Main intake][elig] · [Revisit][revisit] · [Cluster guard][min-signals] · [Family classifier][family] · [Oracle decision][oracle] · [Source identity decision][family-adr]
4. **Compare like events.** At each cutoff, start with native and feed-augmented evidence that was observable then. Link the article to candidates through the flattened array, candidates to minting identities through `CANDIDATE_ID`, and merges through the ordered decision ledger and target ID. Independently match the movement across candidates, including earlier deferred or rejected candidates. Compute the native minus augmented time for the **same** qualifying candidate or successful promotion, separately. A simulated or reviewer-qualified earlier time is a counterfactual estimate. When one side has not reached the event by cutoff, report it as censored with follow-up time. An earlier publisher URL or a later trend label alone cannot establish earlier discovery. [Candidate writer][candidate-write] · [Promotion writer][mint] · [Candidate states][states]
5. **Check an active trend at the article's arrival.** Require a prior trend mint and the latest `FCT_TREND_LIFECYCLE_LEDGER` row with `EVALUATED_AT <= observation`. The sweep treats absent lifecycle state as `NEW` and excludes `DORMANT` and `RETIRED`. Use the latest trend vector actually written by that time and a signal with `EMBEDDED_AT <= observation`. The production sweep uses a 72-hour embedding window, similarity ≥0.35, up to 20 eligible neighbors per trend, and excludes already linked pairs. `STG_ATTRIBUTION_BATCHES.POOLS` and `SUBMITTED_AT` show which IDs the job actually considered. Its later batch collection writes only accepted in-pool IDs. Distinguish first receipt, when the pair could be considered, the actual link and a reviewer's independent judgment. [Sweep constants and candidates][attr-pool] · [Active trend selection][attr-select] · [Batch pool DDL][attr-batch] · [Batch validation][attr-validate]

On November 20, `INFORMATION_SCHEMA.TABLES.RETENTION_TIME` was **one day** on the staging signals, candidates and attribution batches, and **21 days** on the signals, trend identities, links, promotion and lifecycle facts. These settings cover historical versions. Older surviving rows can still be read. Once a staging snapshot ages out, current claims, deferrals, merges and promotion pointers cannot reproduce the full pool at a prior cutoff. Today's enrichment vector, current dashboard state, latest narrative's `ORIGINALLY_SURFACED_AT`, or a fact embedded after the cutoff would introduce later information. Keep poll observations, as-of eligibility and adjudications in the pilot record while the relevant versions are available.
