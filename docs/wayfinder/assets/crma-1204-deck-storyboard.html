Trend Tree briefing storyboard

Thirteen slides for the executive briefing. Each card is one slide: its title with the accent word, the one sentence it lands, and the visual or number behind it. Build notes sit beside each card.

Numbers are the 2026-10-06 snapshot and get refreshed the day before presenting. Future Plans items map one to one to the roadmap page.

Decisions to react to

  1. Thirteen slides, not twelve. A title slide plus a close needs one more slot than the charting shape had. Slides 2 and 3 fold into one if twelve is a hard target.
  2. Every slide carries its sentence. The sentence under each title is rendered on the slide. That is the narration a forwarded reader gets. Speaker notes beyond it are a build detail.
  3. The tree is the chapter marker. Slide 4 shows the full tree. Slides 5 to 7 repeat it small beside the title with one layer lit, replacing May's green tick.
  4. The three layers keep the May names. Discovery & Ingestion, Distillation & Promotion, and Lifecycle become the roots, trunk and crown. The third is renamed Enrichment & Lifecycle because naming and profiling is most of the crown's work.
  5. Two-tone titles, one payload word. Exactly one word per title in the accent green, as in the showcase deck.
  6. The worked example is one slide. May's Signal, Decision and Payoff rhythm compressed into a single root-to-crown trace. It waits on the trend pick in CRMA-1201.
  7. Current state shows only what a strategist sees on ATLAS today. The daily prediction score is current; the prediction engine with a verdict is future. Product matching is current; the external catalogue is future.

What this outline asks of the open tickets

Design the tree visual (CRMA-1205) needs two states: the full tree for slides 4 and 13, and a small glyph with one layer lit for slides 5 to 7. The layer labels are the slide titles, word for word. The funnel numbers sit on the full state only.
Decide: the worked-example trend (CRMA-1201) gets slide 8's shortlist criteria: promoted after May 2026, at least three publishers, more than one source family, legible to a non-technical executive, and ideally carrying both nearest content and a matched product.
Build and publish the deck (CRMA-1206) inherits decisions 2, 3 and 5 as layout rules and each slide's rules as copy rules.
